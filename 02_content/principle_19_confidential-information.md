Principle 19: Confidential Information

The Code expects Market Participants to clearly and effectively identify Confidential Information and to appropriately limit access to it. In practice, this means knowing which information is sensitive, such as client orders, positions and trading activity, and restricting who inside the firm can see it. 
ecb

Why it matters: Clients trust dealers with sensitive information. If it spreads, others can trade ahead of the client, the client may get worse prices, and the firm’s reputation and market integrity suffer.

Example: A corporate client calls a sales dealer with a large EUR/USD order. The dealer treats it as confidential. Only colleagues who need it to execute or manage the order see it. It is not mentioned in a chat room or shared with unrelated desks.

Uncertainty: I couldn’t check the full current text, including the 2021 guidance on handling trading information internally. The example is illustrative, not Code wording. Disclosure to outside parties falls under Principle 20, not Principle 19.

Question 1
What does Principle 19 of the FX Global Code expect from Market Participants?

A) To publish all client trading information so the market stays transparent
B) To clearly and effectively identify Confidential Information and appropriately limit access to it
C) To share Confidential Information freely between all desks within the same firm
D) To delete all records of client orders immediately after execution

Correct answer: B. Principle 19 calls for Market Participants to clearly and effectively identify Confidential Information and appropriately limit access to it.

Question 2 (scenario)
A corporate client tells a sales dealer about a large EUR/USD order it plans to place later today. Which action is most consistent with Principle 19?

A) Post the details in an inter-bank chat room to gauge market appetite
B) Tell a friendly dealer at another bank so they can “stay out of the way”
C) Share the details only with colleagues who have a legitimate role in handling the order
D) Pass the details to the proprietary trading desk so it can position ahead of the order

Correct answer: C. Principle 19 requires access to Confidential Information to be appropriately limited, so only those with a legitimate role in the order should see it.

Question 3
Which of the following would a Market Participant treat as Confidential Information under the Code?

A) A central bank’s publicly announced interest rate decision
B) A benchmark fixing rate already published by the administrator
C) Details of a client’s pending order, such as its size, direction and timing
D) A general comment in a news article that the euro has weakened

Correct answer: C. The Code treats information about the past, present or future trading activity or positions of a firm or its clients as Confidential Information, so a client’s pending order must be identified and protected.
