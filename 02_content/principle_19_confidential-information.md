Principle 19: Confidential Information

What it requires: Market Participants should clearly and effectively identify Confidential Information and appropriately limit who can access it. In practice, know what counts as confidential, such as information about the past, present and future trading activity or positions of your firm or its clients. Then make sure only people who genuinely need it can see it.

Why it matters: If client or firm trading information spreads too widely, others can trade ahead of it or move prices. That harms clients and trust in the market.

Example: A sales trader sees a client’s large EUR/CHF order. The client’s identity and order size should not reach the proprietary trading desk through chat or shared screens. Access stays with people who need it, such as for risk management.

Uncertainty: I couldn’t check the Code’s full supporting text, so I may have missed details. Also, when disclosure is acceptable is covered by Principle 20, not Principle 19, so I left it out.
Question 1

According to Principle 19 of the FX Global Code, what should Market Participants do with Confidential Information?

A. Share it freely within the firm, as long as it is not shared outside the firm
B. Clearly and effectively identify it and appropriately limit access to it
C. Delete it as soon as a trade has been confirmed and settled
D. Make it available to all clients on request so that everyone has the same information

Correct answer: B. Principle 19 asks Market Participants to clearly and effectively identify Confidential Information and to appropriately limit access to it, not to share it broadly or delete it.

Question 2

A sales trader at a bank receives a large EUR/CHF order from a client. A colleague on the bank’s proprietary trading desk asks over chat, “Anything big coming in today?” What is the best response under Principle 19?

A. Share the size of the order but not the client’s name
B. Share the details, because both traders work for the same bank
C. Not share the order details, because access to Confidential Information should be limited to those who need it
D. Share the details only if the colleague promises to keep them private

Correct answer: C. Principle 19 expects firms to limit access to Confidential Information appropriately, and a colleague who merely asks does not have a need to know.

Question 3

Which of the following best describes what counts as Confidential Information in the FX Global Code?

A. Only the identity of a client, never details of the trades
B. Only information that has been explicitly marked “confidential” in writing
C. Only information about orders that have not yet been executed
D. Information relating to the past, present or future trading activity or positions of a Market Participant or its clients, including related sensitive information

Correct answer: D. The Code defines Confidential Information broadly, as information about past, present and future trading activity or positions of the firm or its clients, so it is not limited to names, labels or unexecuted orders.
