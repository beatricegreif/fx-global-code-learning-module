Principle 20: Don’t disclose Confidential Information externally, except in specific circumstances

What it requires: Confidential Information (for example, client orders or your desk’s positions; see Principle 19) may be disclosed externally only in specific circumstances. The Code’s examples, which are not exhaustive, are:

with the client’s or counterparty’s consent
to brokers, platforms or other agents, as far as needed to execute, process, clear or settle a trade
where law or a regulator requires it
to a central bank acting for policy purposes
to advisors who protect the information equally well

Why it matters: Leaked client information can move prices against clients and erodes trust in the market.

Example: A trader at another bank asks who is buying EUR/USD today. Naming your client is not allowed. Passing the order details to your executing broker is.

Question 1

Which of the following is given in Principle 20 as an example of when Confidential Information may be disclosed externally?

A. To any other Market Participant that asks for it and promises to keep it confidential.
B. To advisors or consultants, on condition that they protect it in the same manner as the disclosing Market Participant.
C. To a Client’s competitors, where this supports price discovery.
D. To the press, if the information is likely to become public anyway.

Correct answer: B. Principle 20 lists disclosure to advisors or consultants as acceptable only on the condition that they protect the information as the disclosing Market Participant would.

Question 2

A trader at another bank calls you before a major fixing and asks: “I hear you have a large USD seller at the fix. Who is it, and how much?” What does Principle 20 point to as the right response?

A. Name the Client but not the amount.
B. Share both details, since the caller promises not to trade on them.
C. Decline to disclose, because none of the Principle 20 circumstances applies: there is no consent, it is not needed to execute or settle a trade, and no law or authority requires it.
D. Share both details, since both banks are Market Participants.

Correct answer: C. Principle 20 says Confidential Information should be disclosed only under specific circumstances, such as consent, execution or settlement needs, or legal and regulatory requirements, and none of these applies here.

Question 3

According to Principle 20, under what condition may a Market Participant choose to share its own prior positions or trading activity?

A. As long as the information does not reveal any other party’s Confidential Information and is not shared to disrupt market function, hinder price discovery, or further manipulative or collusive practices.
B. Only with the prior approval of a regulator.
C. Never, because a firm’s own positions are always Confidential Information.
D. Only once the positions have been closed for at least 24 hours.

Correct answer: A. Principle 20 allows firms to share their own positions and trading activity on exactly these conditions: it must not expose others’ Confidential Information, disrupt the market, or support manipulative or collusive practices.
