Principle 11: Pre-Hedging

Pre-hedging means a dealer trades to manage the risk of a client order it expects to receive and may accept, before the order is executed. Principle 11 says Market Participants should only pre-hedge client orders when acting as principal, and should do so fairly and with transparency. It should not be used in a way that disadvantages the client or disrupts the market.

Why it matters: Clients need to trust that a dealer’s risk management does not come at their expense, and they need to know how their orders are handled.

Example: A client asks a bank for a large EUR/USD buy. Acting as principal, the trader buys some EUR in a measured way to cover the expected risk, consistent with the bank’s disclosed order-handling practices. The trader does not buy aggressively to push the price up before quoting.

Question 1

According to Principle 11, when may a Market Participant pre-hedge a client order?

A) Whenever the client has not explicitly forbidden it
B) Only when acting as agent for the client
C) Only when acting as principal, and fairly and with transparency
D) Never, because pre-hedging is prohibited under the Code

Correct answer: C. Principle 11 states that Market Participants should only pre-hedge client orders when acting as principal, and should do so fairly and with transparency.

Question 2 (scenario)

A salesperson at a bank agrees to execute a large EUR/USD buy order for a client as the client’s agent. Before routing the order, the trader suggests buying EUR for the bank’s own book to “get ahead of” the order. What does the Code say?

A) This is acceptable if the trade is small relative to the order
B) This is acceptable if the bank discloses it after the trade
C) This is acceptable if the bank gives the client the same average price
D) This is not consistent with Principle 11, because pre-hedging applies only when acting as principal

Correct answer: D. Principle 11 limits pre-hedging to situations where the Market Participant acts as principal, so trading ahead of an order it handles as agent falls outside it.

Question 3

Which of the following describes a legitimate purpose of pre-hedging under the Code?

A) Managing the risk of an anticipated client order in a way that aims to benefit the client and limit adverse market impact
B) Moving the market in the dealer’s favour before quoting the client
C) Profiting from knowing the client’s order size before it is executed
D) Discouraging the client from asking other dealers for quotes

Correct answer: A. The Code treats pre-hedging as legitimate risk management for anticipated client orders and not as a way to disadvantage the client or disrupt the market.
