Principle 17: Last Look

Last look lets a liquidity provider make a final check on a client’s trade request before accepting or rejecting it at the quoted price. Principle 17 requires firms that use it to be transparent: they must clearly disclose to clients how it works and have well-documented policies. As I understand the 2021 update, last look is a risk control (validity and price checks), not a way to profit from information in the client’s request, and the hold time should be no longer than necessary.

Why it matters: Clients cannot judge execution quality if they don’t know when or why their trades may be rejected.

Example: Before streaming prices to a new corporate client, a salesperson gives them a written summary of the desk’s last look policy: which checks apply, how long the window is, and how rejections are handled.

Uncertainty: I’m confident about the transparency core. Please verify the exact wording of the 2021 additions (hold time, use of trade information) against the published Code before release.
Question 1

What is the purpose of last look under Principle 17 of the FX Global Code?

A. To give the liquidity provider a free option to reject trades that move against it
B. To act as a risk control that verifies the validity and/or the price of a trade request
C. To let the liquidity provider see the client’s trade direction before deciding whether to hedge
D. To guarantee the client the best available price in the market

Correct answer: B
Principle 17 treats last look as a risk control for checking validity and price, not as a source of trading advantage.
Question 2 (scenario)

A liquidity provider receives a client’s request to buy EUR/USD at the quoted price. While the request is held in the last look window, the dealer buys EUR/USD in the interbank market to cover the position the client is about to create, and only then accepts the client’s trade. How does this fit with Principle 17?

A. It is acceptable, as long as the window is under one second
B. It is acceptable, because the dealer is protecting the firm from market risk
C. It is not consistent with the Code, because trade request information should not be used to trade during the last look window
D. It is acceptable, provided the client is a corporate and not a financial institution

Correct answer: C
Under Principle 17, last look should not be used to exploit the client’s trade request information, so trading on it during the window conflicts with its purpose.
Question 3

Which of the following best reflects what Principle 17 expects of a firm that uses last look?

A. It must be transparent with clients about how last look works, and keep the hold time no longer than necessary for its risk checks
B. It only needs to explain last look to a client after that client’s first rejected trade
C. It may keep its last look practices confidential, as they are proprietary risk controls
D. It must always use the same fixed hold time, regardless of its risk checks

Correct answer: A
Principle 17 is built on transparency about last look practices, and the 2021 update says the hold time should be limited to what the risk checks need.
