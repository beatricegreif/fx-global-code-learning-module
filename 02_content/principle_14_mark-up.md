Principle 14: Mark-up

Mark-up added to client transactions by firms acting as principal should be fair and reasonable. Firms should have policies and procedures so that prices charged to clients are fair and reasonable given market conditions. They should also monitor whether actual mark-up practice matches those policies. 
A New Beginning for Fair & Transparent FX Markets
 +2

Why it matters: Clients often can’t see the mark-up inside the price. Fair, monitored pricing protects trust and the firm’s reputation.

Desk example: A client asks you to sell 50M USD/JPY and confirm details later. Filling it further from the executed rate, though still within the day’s range, to add extra mark-up is cited as inappropriate. Price it by your documented mark-up policy instead.

Question 1
According to Principle 14, what should apply to mark-up on client transactions by a firm acting as principal?

A. It should be fair and reasonable.
B. It should be zero unless the client agrees in advance.
C. It should be identical for every client.
D. It should be approved by the client for each trade.

Correct answer: A. Principle 14 says mark-up applied to client transactions by market participants acting as principal should be fair and reasonable.

Question 2 (scenario)
A client asks you to sell USD 50M of USD/JPY and to confirm the details later. Which action is consistent with Principle 14?

A. Choose a fill level from the day’s range that gives the firm the highest mark-up.
B. Apply mark-up according to the firm’s documented policy, and explain the factors behind it if the client asks.
C. Refuse to give the client any information about mark-up, however they ask.
D. Apply a higher mark-up because the client seems less experienced with FX.

Correct answer: B. The Code treats a higher mark-up set off the day’s range as inappropriate, and it expects a best effort to explain the factors behind mark-up if the client requests details.
Question 3
Which approach best reflects what Principle 14 expects of a firm?

A. Relying on clients to raise complaints about mark-up.
B. Leaving mark-up to each trader’s discretion, with no written policy.
C. Having processes to monitor whether its mark-up practices match its policies and procedures.
D. Setting mark-up solely by the day’s trading range.

Correct answer: C. Principle 14 expects firms to have policies and procedures for fair and reasonable pricing, plus processes to monitor whether actual mark-up practice is consistent with them.
